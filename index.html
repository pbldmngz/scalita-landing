Harmony training

Record one line. Sing a second over the top of it and hear them together. Up to four parts, and a part can be a take from the microphone, a file you already had, or a scale the app plays.

  • Say what you are singing Tell Scalita that a part sits a third, a fifth or an octave above the bottom voice and it judges the interval you were aiming for. Without that, a fourth sung where a third was meant reads as perfectly in tune, because it is.
  • Tuned the way singers tune Intervals are judged against just intonation rather than equal temperament, so a major third is allowed to sit about fourteen cents flat. Which is where a singer actually puts it.
  • A picture you can keep Four contours drawn over each other on a named pitch grid, a thread of light where two voices lock, and one image of the finished harmony that can leave the phone.

Recording is free. So are four parts, and any length. Pro only comes in if you want to keep more than one saved harmony, and you can always build another in full and keep that one instead.

And when you want to go further

The rest of the app, in four pieces. None of it is in your way on day one.

Warmups that answer back

Find My Warmup asks how your voice feels today and what you want to work on, then picks the routine and sets the tempo. Adaptive Mode drops the plan entirely: after each exercise you say how it went and the next one adjusts. Tell it you have ten minutes and it paces the session to fit, cooldown included.

Hear yourself properly

Put earphones on and a live trail shows where you land on every note, green on pitch and amber when you drift. Pitch targeting narrows it to one key at a time, and now it keeps your takes: the audio, the pitch line and what the keyboard was doing, so you can open one next week and hear whether anything moved.

Live pitch monitor Pitch targeting Saved practice takes

Registers most apps ignore

Most warmup apps stop at chest and head voice. Set a range for each register you actually use and three dedicated routines open up: the rattle under your chest voice, the flute tone above your range, and the whistle above that. Mark your voice break too, and exercises build around it.

Make your own

Build scales and routines from scratch: intervals, syllables, tempos, starting notes. Or sing or play a pattern into the Scale Detector and it writes the notes down for you. Rebuilt for 1.9.0, it needs about half as many corrections on real singing, and it flags the notes that were a coin toss so you know where to look.
