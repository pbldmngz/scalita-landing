New

Accuracy Training

Four ways to sing in tune. Every one of them trains your ear and your voice together: hear where the note is, then put your voice right there.

  • Pitch targeting Tap a key to hear it, then sing it. Hold the key and it keeps repeating while a live line shows how close you are.
  • Harmony training Record one part, then sing the next over it, up to four, all of them yours. Say which interval you are aiming for and it judges that one, in just intonation, the way singers actually tune.
  • Listen & Repeat The piano plays a phrase, then goes quiet while you sing it back. Leaps and runs from songs you know, sung from memory.
  • Humming Bird A game where you steer a held note through gaps a few cents wide.

Also new: with earphones in, wired or Bluetooth, every scale and routine ends with a score. How much landed in tune, how close the rest was, and one thing to work on. All of it is free. Pro only adds the practice recorder in pitch targeting and room for more than one saved harmony.

And when you want to go further

The rest of the app, in four pieces. None of it is in your way on day one.

Warmups that answer back

Find My Warmup asks how your voice feels today and what you want to work on, then picks the routine and sets the tempo. Adaptive Mode drops the plan entirely: after each exercise you say how it went, and what felt off if something did, and the next one adjusts. Tell it you have ten minutes and it paces the session to fit, cooldown included.

Hear yourself properly

Put earphones on and a live trail shows where you land on every note, green on pitch and amber when you drift. With earphones, wired or Bluetooth, every scale and routine then ends with a score: how much landed in tune, how close the rest was, and one thing to work on. Pitch targeting narrows it to one key at a time and keeps your takes: the audio, the pitch line and what the keyboard was doing, so you can open one next week and hear whether anything moved.

Registers most apps ignore

Most warmup apps stop at chest and head voice. Set a range for each register you actually use and three dedicated routines open up: the rattle under your chest voice, the flute tone above your range, and the whistle above that. Mark your voice break too, and exercises build around it.

Make your own

Build scales and routines from scratch: intervals, syllables, tempos, starting notes. Or sing or play a pattern into the Scale Detector and it writes the notes down for you. Since its rebuild it needs about half as many corrections on real singing, and it flags the notes that were a coin toss so you know where to look.
